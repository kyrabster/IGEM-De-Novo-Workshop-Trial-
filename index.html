HKU iGEM · De novo design studio

Design a protein that switches off inflammation.

A chronic wound keeps triggering an immune alarm. The alarm travels through a receptor called MD2. If we can build a small protein that grabs MD2 and covers the right spot, the alarm never fires — and the wound gets a chance to heal.

No protein exists that does this. So we will invent one: pick the target surface, let generative AI sculpt a backbone, then push it through a four-stage filter until only the best candidate survives.

What the models are actually doing

RFdiffusion starts from random noise shaped like a protein and removes the noise step by step until a plausible backbone appears — the same idea as an image generator, in 3D.

ProteinMPNN keeps that backbone fixed and solves a puzzle: which amino acid sequence would most likely fold into it?

Stage 2.1 · fold quality (pLDDT) · 5 min Done

Throw away anything that won't fold

A designed sequence only works if it actually curls into a stable shape. AlphaFold gives every design a per-residue confidence called pLDDT, scored 0–100. Keep anything at 70 or above. Discard the rest.

Confidence is not the same as quality — but a design nobody can fold has no chance at all, and it is the cheapest thing to check first.

Does it actually grip MD2?

Boltz-1 folds the binder together with MD2 and reports ipTM, a 0–1 score for how confident it is about the interface. Keep anything at 0.60 or above.

Stage 2.3 · off-target safety (MMseqs2) · 5 min Done

Could it grab the wrong protein?

MMseqs2 compares every candidate against the entire human proteome. If a design looks like a piece of a real human protein, it might bind somewhere we never intended. Discard any candidate with one or more off-target hits.

Stage 2.4 · immunogenicity (MHCflurry) · 5 min Done

Will the immune system attack your medicine?

Our body displays fragments of every protein on MHC molecules so T-cells can inspect them. MHCflurry predicts which fragments get displayed. Discard any candidate with one or more strong MHC binders — those risk an immune reaction.

How UCB chose that

Upper Confidence Bound scores every position as μ + c √(ln N / n) — how good it looks so far (μ), plus a bonus for being under-explored (the square-root term). Then it picks the highest score. That is how you balance trying new things against using what already works.

Strong enough to build
ΔG ≤ −8.0=worth synthesising

Every mutation we tried was tested by a model, not a pipette. A wet lab would take weeks to test this many variants — the model narrows thousands of options down to a handful worth testing for real.

ScSearching a space by balancing exploration and exploitation is Bayesian optimisation
